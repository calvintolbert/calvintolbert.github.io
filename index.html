About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the School of Operations Research and Information Engineering at Cornell University, advised by David Bindel. I am on track to graduate in 2028. Prior to my Ph.D., I earned a master's degree in Management Science and Engineering and a bachelor's degree in Mathematics and Classics, both from Stanford University.

My research focuses on probabilistic machine learning, with an emphasis on numerical and computational methods with finite-time convergence guarantees. Recently, I've been working on Bayesian matrix completion, non-log-concave sampling, non-convex global optimization, and Gaussian process computation for semiparametric kriging and Bayesian optimization. I am also involved with a project through the Office of Naval Research on AI-based preference learning for military logistics.

My teaching interests are mainly in mathematical methods, but I also spent the summer of 2025 working with Professors Eric Gentsch and Jack Muckstadt on developing a packet of course materials to accompany Muckstadt's textbook Principles of Supply Chain Management. In spring 2026, I served as a teaching assistant for that course and delivered a lecture on the bullwhip effect.
